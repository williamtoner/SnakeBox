Each cell is one of the 8192 corners of the 13-dimensional cube, arranged so that side-by-side cells differ in a single coordinate. Colour shows how far along the snake a vertex is, from violet at the start to orange at the end.

What is a snake in the box?

Take an n-dimensional cube and walk along its edges from corner to corner. The rule: you may never step next to a corner you have already visited, except the one you just came from. The longest such walk is called a snake.

The question goes back to W. H. Kautz in 1958, who needed it for error-detecting codes. The answer is known exactly only up to eight dimensions. Above that, every number is a record waiting to be beaten.

How this one was found

The previous record, Tom Taylor's 2938, is built recursively from snakes in lower dimensions, so it splits into pieces that each fill a 9- or 10-dimensional subcube. Holding the rest of the snake fixed, I cleared one piece at a time and rebuilt it with a GPU simulated-annealing search, keeping it whenever it came back longer. Three rebuilds took it from 2938 to 2944.

An exact constraint-programming search over a 50 to 60 edge window of one of those snakes then found a reroute two edges longer: 2946.

    Verify any snake
